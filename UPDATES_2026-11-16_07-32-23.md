# UPDATES 2026-11-16 07:32 — date-based, orbit-aware solar-system flights

The solar-system map ("Systemic" regime) no longer treats bodies as motionless points joined by a straight line. Positions depend on a launch date, the chemical rocket coasts on real orbits found for that date, the ion drive is a realistic low-thrust spiral, and a launch-window chart shows the fuel needed for every launch date and travel time. The star map is unchanged except for the ion drive's numbers.

`src/physics/kinematicsEngine.ts` was not changed.

## Units and conventions
- Day number: days since 2000-01-01 12:00 UT (J2000). Whole numbers are noon. `dayFromIso`, `isoFromDay`, `todayDay`, `MIN_DAY` (1900-01-01), `MAX_DAY` (2199-12-31).
- Orbit math: AU, days, `MU_SUN = 0.01720209895²` AU³/day². Heliocentric, ecliptic of J2000 (same frame as `scripts/systemic.json`).
- Velocity changes ("fuel budget", delta-v): km/s.
- Scene coordinates on the solar-system map: ecliptic (x, y, z) to scene (x, z, −y), 1 unit = 1 AU.

## Math

### Ephemeris (`ephemeris.ts`)
- Planets: `PLANETS` table = JPL "approximate positions of the planets" (Standish), elements at J2000 plus rates per Julian century; `elementsAt(name, day)`. Earth uses the Earth-Moon barycentre row.
- Small bodies: fixed osculating elements at their epoch; mean anomaly advanced with n = √(μ/a³).
- `solveKepler(M, e)`: Newton iteration (start M + e·sin M, or ±π when e ≥ 0.8).
- `elementsToState`: perifocal position (a(cos E − e), a√(1−e²) sin E), velocity √(μa)/r · (−sin E, √(1−e²) cos E), rotated by argument of perihelion, inclination, node.
- Moon: mean elements round Earth (`moonElements`: L = 218.316° + 13.176396°/d, M = 134.963° + 13.064993°/d, node = 125.045° − 0.0529539°/d, e 0.0549, i 5.145°), added to Earth's position.
- Checked against the JPL Horizons samples already stored in `scripts/systemic.json` (the 101 `orbit_path` points per body; their dates are rebuilt in the test): planets within 0.3% of heliocentric distance, small bodies within 8% (one fixed orbit over up to 250 years), Moon within 5°.

### Lambert and propagation (`lambert.ts`)
- `lambert(r1, r2, tof, mu)`: universal-variable form, zero revolutions, prograde (short way when (r1 × r2)·z ≥ 0). Bisection on z between −4π² (expanded downward for fast hyperbolic trips, floor −4e5) and 4π². Returns v1, v2 or null.
- `propagate(r0, v0, dt, mu, r, v)`: universal Kepler equation solved with bracketed Newton; f and g functions.
- Checks: Hohmann Earth to Mars (258.9 days, +2.94 km/s and −2.65 km/s); round trip lambert then propagate; Perseverance's dates give 3.81 km/s of departure excess speed (real launch energy C3 about 14.5 km²/s²). New Horizons' Earth to Jupiter leg gives 12.59 km/s (real: C3 158 km²/s², 12.57 km/s).

### Chemical rocket (`transfer.ts`)
- `transferCost(origin, dest, departDay, tofDays)`: Lambert between the two bodies' positions; excess speeds v∞ at each end.
- `departureBurn`: from a circular parking orbit at 1.05 body radii: √(v∞² + 2GM/r) − √(GM/r). Bodies without gravity data: v∞.
- `arrivalBurn`: fly-past 0; otherwise loose capture at 1.05 radii: √(v∞² + 2GM/r) − √(2GM/r); bodies without gravity data: v∞ (full rendezvous).
- `porkchop(origin, dest, startDay)`: 96 launch dates × 72 travel times. Date span = clamp(1.25 × synodic period, 400 d, 30 y). Travel times 0.1 to 1.6 × the half-ellipse time for the two bodies' current distances from the Sun. Cached, 8 entries.
- `solveBallistic(query)`: among grid cells within budget, the one with the soonest arrival; then every day (or 1/16 of a column) within one column either side is searched with `fastestOnDay` (first row that fits, bisected against the previous row; also tries shorter than the grid). No cell fits: cheapest cell, `feasible: false`. `pinnedTofDays` flies exactly that date and travel time.
- The Moon is treated as Earth for trips round the Sun.

### Earth and Moon (`solveLunar`)
- Family of Earth orbits with perigee at the parking radius (6,690 km) and perigee speed vp. Time to the Moon's distance (384,400 km) from Kepler's equation (elliptic or hyperbolic). Speed relative to the Moon from vis-viva and angular momentum, Moon on a circular orbit at 1.018 km/s.
- Fastest vp within budget by bisection; minimum is the orbit that just reaches the Moon (4.97 days, 3.25 km/s total).

### Ion drive (`solveSpiral`, `spiralProfile`)
- Edelbaum: Δv = √(v1² + v2² − 2·v1·v2·cos(π/2 · tilt)), v = circular speed at each body's semi-major axis, tilt = angle between the orbit planes. Trip time = Δv / acceleration.
- Path: circular speed falls or rises linearly in time, radius = μ/v², swept angle = ∫v³/μ dt (closed form in `spiralSweep`). The launch waits (up to 1.05 synodic periods) until the spiral ends at the destination's longitude. The small offsets between the ideal circle-to-circle spiral and the real, slightly oval and tilted orbits are blended in linearly so the path starts and ends on the bodies.
- Earth and Moon: Δv = low-orbit speed − Moon's speed (6.7 km/s), time = Δv / acceleration.
- Not counted: getting off the ground, escaping the starting planet, capture at the destination.

### Direct flights (`solveDirect`)
- Fixed-point iteration: travel time for the distance to where the destination will be after that travel time. Uses `KinematicsEngine` and the existing profile builders unchanged, then `withDirectPath` attaches a two-point trajectory.
- Used for fusion, antimatter, warp, and any flight with the Sun at either end (all engines).

### Profiles
- `FlightProfile` gained optional `trajectory` (`kind`, `frame`, `points`, `count`, `paramAt(f)`), and `FlightPhase` gained `'orbit'` and `'spiral'`.
- `buildTableProfile`: sampled profile with linear interpolation; distance = ∫v dt; clock lag = ∫v²/2c² dt. `ballisticProfile` (200 samples), `spiralProfile` (360), `lunarProfile` (96, path drawn as a line that rides with Earth: `frame: 'Earth'`).

## Technical debt and limits
- No gravity assists. New Horizons flown direct needs 11.8 km/s here; the real probe needed 8.9 km/s plus Jupiter.
- No multi-revolution transfers: a ship that cannot afford today's launch waits at the origin instead of loitering round the Sun.
- Lambert solver is prograde only and fails exactly at a half-turn transfer (returns a huge cost near it, which is the real out-of-plane penalty for tilted orbits).
- Ion spiral is Edelbaum's circle-to-circle approximation with a visual blend to the real end points; constant acceleration regardless of distance from the Sun.
- Flights to or from the Sun use the straight-line constant-acceleration model for every engine (a chemical "speed limit" still applies there, though the slider is hidden only for orbit-following flights).
- Small-body positions use one fixed orbit for all dates (up to 8% off over 250 years; Bennu's elements have a 2011 epoch).
- `scripts/systemic.json` still ships its `orbit_path` arrays (unused by the map now, used by `ephemeris.test.ts`); `coords` there are a snapshot with each body at a different date. `scripts/build_data.py` was not changed.
- The launch-window grid computes synchronously (about 0.2 s the first time for a route and date).
- The first `derive` for a chemical flight on a new route or date also builds that grid, because the engine cards show the chemical rocket's time for every route.
- Engine comparison times exclude any wait for a launch window.
